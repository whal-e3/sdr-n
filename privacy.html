OrbitScope privacy policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

Developer: sunhyuk

Privacy contact: ericwhale0@gmail.com

Location

OrbitScope uses location supplied by Android, with your permission, to calculate satellite visibility, pointing and Doppler correction on your device. You can use manual coordinates or the offline map instead. The app stores the selected coordinates and location preference in its private storage. The current app does not send these coordinates to the developer or its catalog/orbit endpoints. New automatic location updates are requested while the app is visible; it does not request Android's background-location permission.

Android and the device's location providers have their own settings and privacy practices.

SDR and received signals

With your USB permission, OrbitScope reads samples from an attached supported SDR. It processes spectrum/IQ views and supported audio/packet signals on your device. Supported NFM and 1200-baud AFSK sessions automatically save bounded demodulated PCM audio in app-private storage when space permits. After an NFM recording ends, the app also attempts to save a derived playable WAV copy in app-private storage if a 256 MiB free-space reserve plus safety margin remains; it retains the original PCM if copying is skipped or fails. RTL-SDR sessions also try to save original complex CU8 I/Q by default, with a bounded session limit and a 512 MiB free-space reserve; this raw-I/Q path is unavailable for HackRF. CRC-verified 1200-baud AFSK or experimental 9600-baud G3RUH AX.25 frames are automatically saved to a bounded JSONL journal starting with the first verified frame. These files can include the selected tracking target, receiver settings, timestamps and observer coordinates; PCM, WAV and packet logs are not raw RF I/Q. The latest 20 decoded frames are also kept in memory for display. The receiver shows whether saving actually succeeded or hit a limit. You can export an original saved file, an automatically saved WAV copy, or a losslessly wrapped on-demand WAV of completed PCM through Saved files, or separately Copy/Save recent packets; an Android document provider selected for export may be local or cloud-based. WAV creation and export do not alter the original PCM. The app does not upload SDR samples, received audio, decoded packets or USB device information by itself, and it does not record your microphone. A manually or explicitly auto-started receiver can continue in the background. On Android 13 and later, you can allow notifications to see Stop reception and Cancel processing in the notification drawer. If you deny notifications, reception can still run, but those drawer controls may be hidden; use the controls in Listen instead.

If you tap Auto listen, the app checks your chosen antenna, location and current receiver catalog while it is visible. If no current signed catalog is available, that explicit tap may request current ISS, SONATE-2, Meteor M2-3 and Meteor M2-4 orbital data from CelesTrak to use with the app's reviewed public receive profiles. It may then start supported audio/packet or configured Meteor M2-x 72k image sessions during predicted passes. The ongoing foreground receiver may continue when the screen is hidden and stops at predicted LOS. Auto listening does not select capture-only raw-I/Q or unsupported image profiles, but a selected RTL-SDR audio/packet session may also save original I/Q as described above. During NFM audio reception, the app may show a provisional PD120 SSTV preview. After the recording ends, it checks the saved PCM and saves a decoded-image PNG with metadata only if PD120 or Martin M1 decoding succeeds. Martin M1 is checked after the recording, not shown as a live preview. For supported Meteor M2-x 72k profiles, the app decodes saved original I/Q into checked frames and partial grayscale images. After reception stops, local background processing automatically examines the finalized recording without reopening USB or uploading data. Its notification allows cancellation; Android can defer processing, including while battery is low. Original recordings remain unchanged, and decoded products and a processing report stay in private app storage. A manually selected experimental CW profile saves original RTL-SDR I/Q and attempts post-pass Morse candidate extraction; the result is not proof of spacecraft identity. Experimental 9k6 G3RUH packet reception uses direct-discriminator data audio and stores only CRC/address-verified packets; it is not selected by Auto listen.

Imported files and saved settings

When you choose an orbit file using Android's file picker, OrbitScope reads that selected file and saves parsed orbital records and their source filename in private app storage. It also stores downloaded/verified catalog data, orbit lookup results and app preferences. The current app does not upload imported files or their filenames. Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer of app-private data are disabled for the app. You can make your own copy by explicitly exporting files.

Network requests

After OrbitScope opens, it checks whether its saved CelesTrak Active orbital list is due for an update. While the app remains open, it repeats the due check about hourly. It does not schedule these checks while closed. A due check requires a network and can download the public Active list over HTTPS without another tap. While no valid Active cache exists, a confirmed pre-HTTP transport failure waits at least one hour before another request; an interrupted or uncertain attempt or unusable HTTP 200 response waits at least two hours. Once a valid Active cache exists, automatic requests are limited to at most once every 24 hours after the previous attempt. This is not a guaranteed hourly or daily download. You can also choose Try fetch in Settings; manual requests have a persistent two-hour minimum interval. Separately, CelesTrak may reject repeat GP downloads until its data next updates. A recognized GP update-limit response delays another automatic attempt for at least two hours; other provider errors stop automatic requests. Settings shows the last loaded orbit update and a manual orbit-file import. The orbit check does not use your location or SDR data. This list is for orbit tracking and does not provide receiver frequencies or decoder profiles. Choosing or preparing a reviewed starter target may request that satellite's NORAD-ID elements from CelesTrak over HTTPS. If a signed-catalog endpoint is configured in a build, the app requests catalog and signature files from that endpoint. The providers necessarily receive the connection's public IP address and request information; their server logging and retention depend on the provider. The app stores any configured service origin and public verification key, not Space-Track account credentials.

This release has no built-in catalog endpoint or in-app connection form. If a separate HTTPS signed-catalog service is configured in another build, its operator controls server logs and retention; consult that operator's policy before connecting. CelesTrak lookups and catalog downloads do not include the app's saved observer coordinates, imported files or SDR captures.

Security

OrbitScope keeps settings, imported orbital records and receive files in Android app-private storage. Network lookups and catalog downloads use HTTPS. Signed catalog data is accepted only after Ed25519 verification against the configured public key; the reviewed starter profiles are built into the app. Android controls device access and the document providers you choose for export. No internet service can be guaranteed completely secure.

Accounts, advertising and analytics

The current app has no app account or login, advertising SDK or developer analytics/crash-report upload integration. Store/platform services operate under their own privacy policies. Revise this policy and the store declarations if these features are added.

If you independently use Space-Track's website in a browser to download orbital data, that browser and Space-Track handle account details, credentials, cookies and requests under their own policies; OrbitScope does not collect or store them. OrbitScope has no in-app Space-Track guide or account connection. Downloaded orbital data enters OrbitScope only when you select the file through Settings → manually import orbit using Android's document picker.

Retention and deletion

Local settings, cached records and app-private receive files remain until removed through Android's Clear storage or uninstall action; OrbitScope does not silently delete older captures to free space. Clearing app storage removes the local catalogs, imported records, raw I/Q, demodulated PCM and packet journals, but does not delete an original orbit file you selected elsewhere or an exported capture in a document provider. Receiver display data is temporary in-process state. Clipboard retention is controlled by Android and other apps with clipboard access. Local Android diagnostic logs are managed by the operating system. The retention of requests sent to CelesTrak or any user-configured catalog service is controlled by that provider.

Permissions and contact

You can deny or revoke location, USB and notification access using Android's controls. Manual coordinates and non-hardware views remain available where applicable; USB reception needs access to the selected SDR. Contact sunhyuk at ericwhale0@gmail.com for privacy questions about OrbitScope.
